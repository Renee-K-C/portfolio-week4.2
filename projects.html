🎶 R&B Music Zone

Classic R&B Legends

The golden voices that shaped R&B — from Motown to the Quiet Storm. These are the artists whose grooves and ballads still hit hard.

Marvin Gaye

1939 – 1984

The Prince of Motown. From "Let's Get It On" to "Sexual Healing," his smooth falsetto and socially conscious lyrics redefined soul music. "What's Going On" remains one of the greatest albums ever made.

Stevie Wonder

1950 – present

A blind prodigy who became one of the most influential musicians in history. "Superstition," "Isn't She Lovely," and "Higher Ground" showcase his genius as a singer, songwriter, and multi-instrumentalist.

Aretha Franklin

1942 – 2018

The undisputed Queen of Soul. Her voice could move mountains — hear it in "Respect," "Think," and "Natural Woman." A symbol of strength, grace, and the civil rights movement.

Luther Vandross

1951 – 2005

The Velvet Voice. Luther ruled Quiet Storm radio with silky ballads like "Here and Now" and "Dance with My Father." Eight Grammy wins and a legacy of romance in every note.

Otis Redding

1941 – 1967

The King of Soul. His raw, fiery delivery on "(Sittin' On) The Dock of the Bay," "Try a Little Tenderness," and "I've Been Loving You Too Long" defined the Stax sound — and influenced every R&B singer who came after.
